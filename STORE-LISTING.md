# North — Chrome Web Store listing copy

Voice: plain, composed, sentence case. Direct without being chummy; no snark
at the reader, no em dashes in store copy. "Tip", never "donate".

Hard rule, learned the hard way: never list platform names in a row. A
submission was rejected for keyword spam (policy reference "Yellow Argon")
over one sentence in the dashboard — "YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels,
Facebook Reels, Snapchat Spotlight, TikTok, adult content, custom websites,
and pages matching your own blocked keywords." A run of brand names reads as
keyword stuffing to review, however accurate it is. So: describe what North
blocks by category, and name a platform only where a feature is genuinely
specific to it (the YouTube controls, the messages-only modes), at most once,
inside a sentence rather than a comma run. The same rule covers the title,
the summary, the manifest description, screenshot captions and every
justification field in the dashboard. In-product copy is not store metadata
and is not affected.

## Detailed description

North blocks everything you don't need to see, short-form videos, distracting sites and keywords, with schedules, daily limits, and an unlock you have to mean. Free forever, with no account and no tracking.

Most blockers paywall the features that actually work, then switch off in two clicks the moment you are tempted. North gives every feature away free, and turning a protection off takes a wait you have to sit through, plus 20 honest words about why. Most urges do not survive the wait.

What North does:

• Short videos, closed. North treats endless vertical video as one category rather than a list of sites: those feeds are hidden where they appear, unreachable by direct link, and never unlockable. A clip someone sends you still plays, just that one. If three people send you something while you were away, all three open; a conversation full of clips does not.

• Rules for the sites that pull you in. Block a site always, on a schedule (work hours, study blocks, evenings), or once a daily time budget runs out. Midnight resets the clock.

• Messages-only modes. A social network can run as an inbox and nothing else: the site opens straight to your conversations while its feed and discovery pages stop loading. Supported on Instagram, LinkedIn, Facebook and X, each switched on separately.

• YouTube on your terms. Choose the topics you actually came for, and the rest fades from feeds and search. The home page, related videos, comments and the sidebar can go too, including the wall of recommendations after a video ends.

• Lockdown mode. For exam week or crunch time: choose a short allowlist, set a duration of up to 24 hours, and everything else stops loading. There is no off switch. It ends when the clock ends.

• Keyword and 18+ blocking. Block pages by what they are about, across every site at once. Adult content is filtered out and can never be unlocked.

• An unlock you have to mean. There is no quick disable. You sit with a breathing timer that only counts while you stay on the page, then write at least 20 honest words to your future self about why you need the site. Pasting is blocked, and keyboard mashing does not count. You set the wait yourself on a slider when you first install North, anywhere from 10 seconds to 2 minutes, and shortening it later takes the same challenge.

• One free minute a day, for your first three days. While North is still new to you, a button in the popup turns everything off for exactly one minute, then switches it all back on by itself. No wait, no challenge, and nothing to remember to undo. After the third day the button stays, and it costs the same wait and challenge as any other way off. Lockdown and focus sessions are never pausable.

• Progress, counted. Every distraction blocked and every focused minute, recorded honestly.

Private by design: no account, no servers, no tracking. Your settings and statistics live in your browser and nowhere else, and you can always uninstall. North's job is to interrupt a habit, not to trap you.

Free for life. Every feature, for everyone, always. No trial, no premium tier, no feature caps. North runs on optional tips, and that is the whole model.

Works in Chrome and other Chromium browsers.

## Single-purpose description (for the privacy / permissions tab)
North is a focus tool that blocks distracting websites and short-form video, and adds deliberate friction before a blocked site can be unlocked. All blocking rules and statistics are stored locally in the browser; North has no servers and collects no data.

## "What does North block?" — the sentence that was rejected

Whichever dashboard field carried the rejected sentence, this replaces it. It
says the same thing by category, with no run of platform names:

North blocks short-form video feeds as a single category, any site you add to your own list, and pages matching keywords you choose. Adult content is filtered out by default and cannot be unlocked.

Longer variant, if the field has room:

North blocks three things: endless short-form video, wherever it appears; the sites you put on your own list, always or on a schedule or once a daily time budget runs out; and pages that match keywords you choose, across every site at once. Adult content is filtered out by default and cannot be unlocked. Nothing is blocked until you choose it, and every rule lives in your browser.

## Permission justifications

Written the same way: one plain sentence of purpose per permission, no lists
of sites, no feature keywords carried over from the description.

- storage: Saves your blocking rules and your statistics in the browser. Nothing is sent anywhere.
- tabs and webNavigation: Lets North see the address a tab is opening so it can decide whether that page is blocked by one of your rules.
- alarms: Ends focus sessions, daily limits and timed unlocks when their time is up.
- idle: Stops counting time towards a daily limit while you are away from the computer.
- notifications: Tells you when a focus session or a lockdown has finished.
- host permissions: Your rules can cover any site you choose, so North has to be able to check the page you are opening against them. It reads the address and the page title, and nothing else.
